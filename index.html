Shape your people. Shape your nation.

02 / The Game

Lead the many. Lose none.

Think Pikmin meets Holdfast: a straggling crowd becomes a drilled little army, and every call you make decides who makes it.

You are the Founder, and you don't stay down. Every rebel you find falls in behind you, and every one is a spare life: get knocked down and you're back up in the nearest of them. The ones you lose are gone for good. Scout ahead on your own if you dare, but out there alone, one more knock ends it.

You can throw a punch, and you will, but the shape of your squad is your real weapon: thread a breach in Column, sweep the open ground in Line, and plant a Square when the cavalry comes thundering in. Change too late and the stragglers pay for it.

Get your people out, bank them toward a nation of a hundred, and go back for more. The island will be waiting.

01

Rebels hide in villages, wander the cane and wait in the Crown's cells. Walk close and they fall in: a growing column of men and women at your back.

Column, Line, Square, on one button. Every enemy's cone tells you what it's doing: green while it looks, amber when it's seen you, red before it fires. Read it, reform, and the danger walks past you.

Every man and woman you bring home counts toward a free nation. The bigger it grows, the more the Crown sends to stop you.

Many hands

The bigger your crowd, the more you can do. Six men on a platform lift a gate. A crew at the capstan winds up a drawbridge. Hands on the pump drain a ford and strand its alligators. A team drags a field gun up to the Redoubt. And there's a hole in the wall only Bonnie fits, with a bell beyond it.

03 / Core Mechanic

Three shapes. One right answer.

Every formation saves your people from something and leaves them open to something else. Your men need time to move into the new shape, so a change made a heartbeat too late still costs you.

Column

Breaches, bridges, mountain ledges

Single file through the gap in the fort wall and along the cliff path. Long and thin: a volley down its length is a disaster.

Line

Open ground

A wide front, fast across the open field. A volley from the side only catches its end. Caught head on, it loses everyone.

Square

Cavalry, bayonets, the boar

A closed ring with you at its heart. It turns a bayonet charge and stops dragoons and a wild boar dead. Slow, and too wide for narrow ground. Against the cannon, no shape helps: get out of its cone.

04 / The Island

An island that fights back

The redcoats hold the forts. The island holds everything else.

!Redcoat lines

"Make ready! Present!" Muskets level, their lane on the ground turns from amber to red, and you have a heartbeat to get out of it.

!Dragoons

They sweep the open ground in threes. Nothing in your squad can touch them, except Bonnie.

!Cannon batteries

A fire cone swings across the ground to find you, the gunners present, and the gun roars. No formation stops a cannonball: get out of the cone, or get to the gun.

!Riflemen and grenadiers

Green-jacketed marksmen who punish standing still. Grenades that sit in a red ring, fizzing, before they burst.

!The eagle

It circles the mountain pass and stoops on the straggler. Men thrown from the ledge vanish into the fog below.

!Alligators

One lies in every ford, patient and impossible to kill. Wading slows everyone, in any formation. You'll hear the hiss before the jaws.

!The fer-de-lance

It waits in the cane. Walk your people round it, or count the cost.

!Man traps

Steel jaws hidden on the plantation paths. Look before you lead anyone through.

!The gaoler

A bearded brute in a foul temper, with a cudgel that sends your whole squad flying and the key to the cells on his belt. Knock him down for it. It takes a while.

!The barracks

Fight within earshot of it and the bugle sounds: squads of line infantry turn out to hunt you down. Go low, and go round.

!The wild boar

It charges your squad on sight and scatters everyone it hits, unless it meets a Square.

Bonnie, a loyal dog at your heel

The only thing on the island a dragoon fears

  • Bonnie follows you everywhere and goes for any redcoat who comes near your people.
  • She runs down fleeing snipers and grenadiers, and she is the only thing that can bring down a dragoon.
  • She's terrified of alligators, snakes and traps. Listen for her warning growl.
  • Second player: Bonnie. Hand her to a friend on a controller. She stays tethered to the Founder, the camera pulls out to keep her in frame, every redcoat hunts her too, and if she falls one of your people gives his life for hers. Only she fits through the gaps in the walls, and she is the only one who can ring the bell beyond them.

05 / Ways to Play

Four ways to take the field

Follow the Founder from the cove to Kingston, push your luck on an endless island, or settle it on the battlefield, against the computer or a friend on the same sofa.

MODE 01REBUILT

Campaign

Ten levels across the island, from the Cove Landing to Kingston Harbour, and none of them a corridor: a village on its terrace above the beach, three cane fields climbing to a mill, a causeway through the swamp, a river in flood, a hill in three belts, a town on a slope. Dusk at the mill, rain on the Cane Road, lamplight at the magazine, fog on the Mountain Pass, and an ending worth the march. Bring a friend: Bonnie takes a second controller.

MODE 02NEW WEAPONS

Skirmish

A freshly made island every trip. Go back inland for more men, again and again, harder every time, until one trip is too many. Your score is how far you got. Save nearly everyone on the right campaign levels and the Founder takes a weapon with him: a claymore from the Smugglers' Harbour, a flintlock from the Sugar Mill, timed grenades from the Powder Magazine and a long rifle from the Redoubt.

MODE 03NEW

1 Player Battle

Take on the computer at Easy, Normal or Hard. It plays by your rules: it forms Square against your horse, waits for your line to fire, and comes for your commander the moment you look away.

MODE 04UPDATED

2 Player Battle

Couch versus on one screen, keyboard against gamepad, in the spirit of the classic 16-bit field battles. River, Chasm or Desert, drawn at random. Lose your commander and you lose the battle, and a sniper can see the whole way across the field: keep yours behind his rock. Built for Steam Remote Play Together too, so the sofa can be two sofas.

Your army on the battlefield

CommanderLineBayonetsCavalryCannonSniperBrawlerGrenadier

Steer one unit at a time, each with its own readiness bar. Hold fire and the cannon's marker walks out, then let go. Line up your sniper and a ring closes on the man he's about to hit, from anywhere on the field; both players can see it, so that man had better move. Cross the bridge in Column, charge in Line, and nobody fires from the river. And every field has powder kegs: one good shot and both armies scatter.

07 / Setting

From County Down, 1798

"They buried the Founder with his sword and his colours. The grave did not hold him."

The history

In the summer of 1798 the United Irishmen rose against the Crown, and in County Down the rising ended at Ballynahinch. The rebels who survived were hanged, jailed, transported or scattered, and some of them ended up across the ocean, on the Crown's sugar islands. Forged in Formation begins there.

The voices

The Founder, his followers and the redcoats who hunt them are voiced by a young Northern Irish cast. Every order, rallying cry and line of narration was recorded here at home for the game.

Forged in Formation is a work of fiction inspired by the 1798 rebellion and the Irish rebels who ended up in the Caribbean after it. It depicts colonial warfare and the plantation system, including the man traps planters set for people escaping slavery. The fighting is stylised and bloodless: men are knocked down, not torn apart.

08 / Also from 3BL - in development

Defend the Drift

The Anglo-Zulu War, 1879

Planned for 2027

Teaser: Isandlwana, Rorke's Drift and Gingindlovu, with the Zulu army's call and then Men of Harlech. Captured in engine from the current build, October 2026.

The rest of the war: Hlobane, Kambula and Ulundi, with the Zulu army's call and then The British Grenadiers, the Royal Scots Fusiliers' march, at Ulundi. Captured in engine from the current build, October 2026.

Our second game is the serious one. Defend the Drift is a battle game of the Anglo-Zulu War for one or two players: one side commands the British garrison, the other the Zulu army.

Six battles, the whole campaign from the first British defeat to the last Zulu one, fought on their real ground and following their day as it went: the eclipse over Isandlwana, the hospital burning through the night at Rorke's Drift, Wood's horsemen riding for their lives down the Devil's Pass at Hlobane, Buller's horsemen drawing the uNgobamakhosi on at Kambula the next day, the mist lifting off the laager at Gingindlovu, and the great square on the plain before oNdini at Ulundi, the last battle of the war.

Between and after the fighting, the people who were there speak: every battle opens with a line from the record of 1879, its worst moment is told in the words of the time, and it ends in silence with a passage from each side, a British writer and a Zulu voice. Every line was checked against the original.

Where it stands, October 2026

  • Two players on one screen, splitting when the commanders part, or a screen each over a LAN or online through Steam.
  • One player against the computer on either side, or two together against it.
  • Lead from overhead or through your commander's eyes: fire the officer's revolver, fight with the induna's iklwa, or man a Gatling yourself.
  • The Zulu army's horns of the buffalo, the British volley by ranks, and Gatlings, 9-pounders and rockets in the laager.
  • Hold the buildings: walk a commander into the hospital or the storehouse and his men take its windows and loopholes, until the thatch above them catches.
  • Every squad has its nerve. Losses and being outnumbered close up wear it down, and a broken squad falls back until its commander reaches it and rallies it, on either side.
  • Warriors climb the mealie-bag walls to get at the garrison.
  • At Kambula an ibutho kept waiting under the guns, or ridden at by Buller's horsemen, may charge alone before the horns are ready, as the uNgobamakhosi did in 1879.
  • At Ulundi the 17th Lancers wait inside the square with their lances, and ride out when the attack breaks.
  • Hlobane is won by getting away: get the horsemen off the mountain by its one steep pass and across the plain before the Zulu army closes in. Men whose horses fall are taken up behind their comrades, riding double.
  • Men carry the sick out of the burning hospital and the mealie bags into the redoubt, runners bring ammunition up to the line, and the guns have their crews in the Royal Artillery's blue.
  • When a battle ends the side that held the field rests where it stands and the other walks away. Nobody cheers.
  • Mounted men in the dress their corps wore in 1879: Buller's Frontier Light Horse in yellow-brown cord and the red-banded slouch hat, colonial horse, burghers, Mounted Infantry, the Natal Native Horse and the 17th Lancers, each on a horse that walks, canters and gallops.
  • Both armies are animated with the same care: the redcoats march and charge with their rifles, the warriors carry shield and iklwa and throw their spears, and every officer and induna signals his orders.
  • A campaign through all six battles in the order they were fought, with records and difficulty settings. It ends the way the war did: a last set of cards in silence, from Cetshwayo's offer of peace after Isandlwana to his capture, and the line that tells you whose voice you have been hearing after every battle.
  • The first battle teaches as you play: a hint at a time under the clock, in your own keys, gone once you have done it.
  • Each battle opens with a flight over the field and ends with a slow pan over the fallen of both sides.
  • Music from the period, every note made in code from the old printed and manuscript settings: The Warwickshire Lads for the 24th, The Campbells Are Coming on the pipes at Gingindlovu, and The Girl I Left Behind Me for the campaign. The Zulu side has no borrowed songs: its own sound is thousands of feet in time, spears on shields and a wordless hum.

Where it began

Defend the Drift began with a family heirloom: a sword bayonet that came down through James's mother's family, the Bouchers. On one side of the blade someone cut the initials J B; on the other, letter by letter, BORE WAR. It got the family reading about the wars in southern Africa, and it is why this game tries to tell that history carefully, from both sides.

In memory of Private Richard Mussen of Belfast, whom his city remembers as one of the garrison.

Defend the Drift is about real battles in which many people died on both sides, and it gives both sides the same care: no gore, no kill counts, and the fallen stay where they fall. Work in progress, captured in engine.
